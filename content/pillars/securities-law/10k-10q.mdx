## Where equity compensation lands in periodic reports

Companies that report under the Exchange Act file an annual report on Form 10-K and quarterly reports on Form 10-Q. Both include financial statements prepared under U.S. GAAP, and stock-based compensation is governed by FASB ASC Topic 718. The most detailed information appears in a stock-compensation footnote, but the expense also flows through the income statement, is added back on the statement of cash flows, and may be discussed in Management's Discussion and Analysis.

## What the footnote discloses

The ASC 718 footnote describes each significant equity plan and the types of awards outstanding, such as options, RSUs, and performance shares. It reports total compensation expense recognized for the period and often breaks it down by award type or income-statement line. A key disclosure is the amount of unrecognized compensation cost tied to unvested awards, together with the weighted-average period over which that cost is expected to be recognized. Activity tables typically roll forward the number of awards granted, vested, exercised, forfeited, and outstanding, along with weighted-average exercise prices and grant-date fair values.

## Valuation and plan capacity

Because most awards are measured at grant-date fair value, the footnote discloses the valuation approach and the assumptions behind it, including expected volatility, expected term, the risk-free interest rate, and expected dividends. Companies also disclose how many shares remain available for future grants under approved plans, which signals potential future dilution. In MD&A, management may explain trends in stock-compensation expense and how equity programs affect operating results and liquidity.

## Professional detail

Under ASC 718, expense for service-based awards is recognized over the requisite service period, usually the vesting period, based on grant-date fair value. Companies may account for forfeitures as they occur or estimate them, and that policy choice is disclosed.

Performance-based awards add complexity: expense recognition depends on whether the performance condition is expected to be met, and market conditions are baked into the grant-date fair value rather than trued up. This distinction affects the timing and reversibility of expense.

The 10-Q footnote is condensed relative to the 10-K but must update material changes, including new grants, modifications, and shifts in assumptions. Excess tax effects from settlements flow through the income tax provision and can add volatility to the effective tax rate.
