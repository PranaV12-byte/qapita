## Who Section 16 covers

Section 16 of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 imposes reporting and disgorgement rules on the insiders of a company that has a class of equity registered under the Act. Three categories are covered: directors, officers holding policy-making roles, and beneficial owners of more than 10 percent of a registered class. These reporting persons must keep the market informed of their holdings and transactions in the company's equity, including derivative positions such as options and RSUs.

## Forms 3, 4, and 5

Reporting happens on three forms filed electronically through EDGAR. Form 3 is the initial statement of beneficial ownership; a new insider files it within ten days of becoming subject to Section 16 (or by the effective date of registration for a company going public). Form 4 reports changes in ownership and is due by the end of the second business day after most transactions, including grants, exercises, purchases, sales, and gifts in many cases. Form 5 is an annual report covering transactions that were exempt from Form 4 reporting or that were inadvertently omitted, and it is due within 45 days after the company's fiscal year end.

## Short-swing profit recovery

Section 16(b) allows the company to recover any profit an insider makes by matching a purchase and a sale (in either order) of company equity within any period shorter than six months. The rule is mechanical: the highest sale price is matched against the lowest purchase price in the window to produce the maximum recoverable profit. There is no requirement to prove the insider used confidential information, which is why insiders and their advisers watch the six-month window closely around equity grants and exercises.

## Professional detail

Beneficial ownership under Section 16 is defined by rule and can reach shares held indirectly through family members or entities where the insider has a pecuniary interest. Derivative securities are reported separately, and many grant and vesting events qualify for exemptions under Rule 16b-3 when approved by the board or an appropriate committee.

The two-business-day Form 4 deadline was tightened by the Sarbanes-Oxley Act. Late filings are disclosed in the company's proxy statement, and a pattern of delinquencies can attract SEC enforcement.

Short-swing matching under 16(b) is enforced by the company or, more often, by a shareholder suing derivatively. Exempt transactions such as qualifying grants generally are not matchable, so proper board approval of grants is a key defense.
