## Reporting equity income on Form W-2

Most equity compensation that produces ordinary income is reported on the employee's Form W-2 under the wage-reporting rules of Internal Revenue Code Section 6051. When a nonqualified stock option is exercised, the spread between the fair market value and the exercise price is wage income. It is included in Box 1 (federal wages), in Boxes 3 and 5 for Social Security and Medicare wages subject to the usual limits, and it is separately shown in Box 12 with Code V. RSU vesting and other ordinary-income equity events are similarly swept into the W-2 wage boxes and subject to withholding.

## Form 3921 for ISO exercises

Incentive stock options receive special tax treatment, so their exercises are reported on Form 3921 rather than as W-2 wages. Under Section 6039, a company that transfers stock to an employee upon exercise of an ISO files Form 3921 for each exercise. The form records the option grant date, the exercise date, the per-share exercise price, the fair market value per share on the exercise date, and the number of shares. This information supports the employee's alternative minimum tax calculation and the eventual determination of qualifying versus disqualifying disposition.

## Form 3922 for ESPP transfers

Form 3922 addresses employee stock purchase plans that qualify under Section 423. When an employee first transfers legal title to shares acquired under such a plan, the company files Form 3922 to report the details needed to compute income and gain on a later sale, including the grant date, purchase date, purchase price, and share values. Like the W-2 and 3921, the recipient copy is generally due by January 31.

## Professional detail

Section 6039 requires both furnishing statements to individuals and filing information returns with the IRS. Only the first transfer of legal title after ESPP purchase triggers a 3922; later transfers are not reportable on that form.

For ISOs, no income tax is withheld at exercise, but the spread is an AMT preference item in the exercise year even though it is not regular-tax income. Form 3921 gives the employee the data to reconcile regular tax and AMT and to establish basis.

Filing deadlines with the government differ from the January 31 recipient deadline. Employers filing many returns must file electronically, and penalties apply for late or incorrect information returns, so year-end equity reconciliation is a significant administrative task.
