## The problem Rule 144 solves

Shares received through equity compensation are often restricted or control securities that cannot simply be sold into the market. Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 requires registration of a sale unless an exemption applies, and Section 4(a)(1) exempts transactions by persons who are not issuers, underwriters, or dealers. Rule 144 is a safe harbor that tells a holder exactly how to resell without being treated as an underwriter, so the Section 4(a)(1) exemption is available. Meeting its conditions removes the registration hurdle for the resale.

## Restricted versus control securities and holding periods

Restricted securities are acquired in unregistered transactions, for example shares issued to employees under Rule 701 or in a private placement. Control securities are held by an affiliate of the issuer, typically a director, officer, or large stockholder, and are restricted by the seller's status rather than how they were acquired. Rule 144 imposes a minimum holding period before restricted securities can be resold: six months if the issuer is a reporting company current in its filings, and one year if it is not. The holding period does not restrict an affiliate's resale of shares that were never restricted, but the other affiliate conditions still apply.

## Conditions for affiliate resales

An affiliate using Rule 144 faces additional conditions even after the holding period. Sales within any three-month period are capped by a volume formula tied to the greater of a percentage of the outstanding class or average weekly trading volume. Sales must meet manner-of-sale requirements, generally routed as ordinary brokers' transactions, and current public information about the issuer must be available. If the affiliate's planned sales exceed set thresholds, a Form 144 notice must be filed with the SEC.

## Professional detail

The volume limitation for an affiliate is generally the greater of one percent of the outstanding shares of the class or the average weekly reported trading volume over a recent four-week window; for many private-company shares only the one-percent test is meaningful.

Rule 144 includes tacking rules that can add prior holders' periods to the current holder's in defined circumstances, and it addresses when the holding-period clock resets. Shares held through certain conversions or as a gift may tack the transferor's period.

A Form 144 is required when an affiliate intends to sell above the applicable share-count or dollar threshold within three months. The notice is a signal of intent, not an approval, and it must be filed at or before the time the sell order is placed.
