## The proxy statement and executive pay

Section 14(a) of the Securities Exchange Act of 1934 governs how public companies solicit shareholder votes. The definitive proxy statement, filed as a DEF 14A on Schedule 14A, is the primary vehicle for disclosing executive compensation. Item 402 of Regulation S-K sets out exactly what must appear, focused on the company's named executive officers. Because equity awards are a large part of senior pay, the proxy is where equity-compensation practices become most visible to investors.

## Core disclosure tables and narrative

The centerpiece is the Summary Compensation Table, which reports each NEO's salary, bonus, stock and option awards, non-equity incentive pay, changes in pension value, and other compensation for the last three fiscal years. Supporting tables show grants of plan-based awards, outstanding equity at year end, and option exercises and stock vested during the year. The Compensation Discussion and Analysis, or CD&A, is a narrative explaining the philosophy, objectives, and decision process behind the numbers, including how performance targets tie to pay.

## Dodd-Frank disclosures

The Dodd-Frank Act added several shareholder-oriented requirements. Say-on-pay is a periodic advisory vote asking shareholders to approve NEO compensation, accompanied by a vote on how often that vote recurs. Pay-versus-performance disclosure requires a table and discussion connecting compensation actually paid to measures of company performance, such as total shareholder return and net income. The pay-ratio disclosure compares the CEO's total compensation to the median compensation of all other employees.

## Professional detail

Equity awards in the Summary Compensation Table are reported at grant-date fair value under ASC 718, which can differ from the amount ultimately realized. The pay-versus-performance table instead uses a "compensation actually paid" concept that adjusts equity values for changes in fair value across the vesting period, so the two figures rarely match.

The CD&A must address clawback policies, hedging and pledging practices, and the use of compensation consultants. It is subject to the say-on-pay vote in substance even though the vote is framed around the overall program.

Pay-ratio methodology gives companies flexibility in identifying the median employee and allows the use of statistical sampling, but the methodology and any material assumptions must be disclosed so investors can assess comparability.
