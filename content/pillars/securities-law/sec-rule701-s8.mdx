## Why equity compensation is a securities matter

Stock options, restricted stock, RSUs, and shares purchased through an ESPP are all securities. Section 5 of the Securities Act of 1933 makes it unlawful to offer or sell a security through interstate commerce unless a registration statement is in effect or an exemption is available. Granting equity to employees is treated as an offer and sale, so every equity program must rest on either registration or a specific exemption. This is separate from the tax rules that govern the same awards.

## Rule 701 for private companies

Rule 701 is the workhorse exemption for privately held companies that do not report to the SEC. It permits sales of securities under written compensatory benefit plans or contracts to employees, directors, officers, general partners, and certain consultants and advisers. The amount of securities that may be sold in any 12-month period is capped by a formula tied to the greater of a fixed dollar figure, a percentage of total assets, or a percentage of the outstanding class of securities.

When aggregate sales in a 12-month period stay below the enhanced-disclosure threshold (currently $10 million), the company generally must provide only a copy of the plan. Once sales cross that threshold, the company must deliver richer disclosure to all Rule 701 recipients in that period, including risk factors and financial statements dated within a set recency window. Rule 701 is a federal exemption only; state blue-sky requirements still apply.

## Form S-8 for public companies

A company that reports under the Exchange Act uses Form S-8 to register shares issued under its equity plans. Form S-8 is a short-form registration available only to eligible reporting companies that are current in their periodic filings. It covers offers and sales to employees, directors, and consultants who provide bona fide services, but it may not be used to raise capital or to register resales by a broad public group. A company files S-8 to cover the pool of shares reserved under a plan and files additional S-8s as the pool grows.

## Professional detail

Rule 701's cap uses the greatest of three measures over a rolling 12-month look-back: a fixed dollar amount, a percentage of the issuer's total assets, and a percentage of the outstanding amount of the class being offered. Options are counted at grant based on the exercise price, not at exercise.

The enhanced-disclosure obligation is a condition of the exemption. If the company crosses the threshold but fails to deliver the required information, the exemption can be lost for the entire offering, exposing the issuer to rescission rights.

Form S-8 automatically incorporates the issuer's Exchange Act reports by reference, so the prospectus is delivered through a plan document and the company's ongoing filings rather than a standalone document. Affiliates reselling S-8 shares must still comply with Rule 144.
