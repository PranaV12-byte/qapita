## Dividends on restricted stock

Because a holder of restricted stock owns actual shares from the grant date, the company can pay real dividends on that stock even while it remains subject to forfeiture. How those dividends are taxed depends on the award's status. If the shares are still unvested and no Section 83(b) election was made, the dividends are treated as additional compensation — ordinary wage income subject to withholding — rather than as dividend income. Once the stock vests, or if an 83(b) election was filed at grant, later dividends are taxed as ordinary dividends and can qualify for preferential qualified-dividend rates when the holding requirements are met.

## Dividend equivalents

An RSU or option confers no ownership before settlement, so there is no share on which a dividend can be paid. To keep holders economically whole, plans may grant dividend equivalent rights that credit an amount matching what a real shareholder would have received. These can be paid in cash or as additional units, and on unvested awards they are compensation income taxed at ordinary rates, not qualified dividends.

## Structuring the timing

The safest design ties dividend equivalents to the underlying award: they accrue and pay only if and when the award vests and settles.

## Professional detail

The central compliance risk with dividend equivalents is IRC § 409A. When equivalents are granted in connection with unvested RSUs or performance units, they should be structured so that payment occurs on the same schedule as the underlying award rather than on the company's ordinary dividend payment dates. Treasury guidance treats a dividend equivalent that is paid at the same time as the underlying stock right as not creating a separate deferral of compensation, whereas an equivalent payable on an independent schedule can constitute its own nonqualified deferred compensation arrangement subject to § 409A's timing rules — with the 20% additional tax and premium interest as the penalty for a defect. For performance-based awards it is common to accrue equivalents on target units and true them up to the shares actually earned, forfeiting equivalents on units that never vest. Under ASC 718, dividends and dividend equivalents paid on awards that ultimately vest are charged to retained earnings, while amounts attributable to awards that are forfeited are reclassified as additional compensation cost. The § 83(b) election remains pivotal for restricted stock, converting post-election distributions from wages into dividend income eligible, when holding-period conditions are satisfied, for the lower rates of IRC § 1(h)(11).
