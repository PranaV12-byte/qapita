## Stock appreciation rights

A stock appreciation right entitles the holder to the increase in a share's value above a base price fixed at grant, measured over the life of the award. If the share value rises, the holder collects that spread; if it does not, the right expires worthless. Unlike an option, a SAR requires no cash outlay to exercise, which is why it is often described as a cashless form of upside participation. Settlement can be in cash or in an equivalent number of shares, and that choice drives the accounting.

## Phantom equity

Phantom stock is a bookkeeping unit that mirrors the value of a real share without conveying ownership. Holders receive the economic benefit  -  appreciation, and sometimes dividend-equivalent credits  -  but have no voting rights and hold no actual stock. Private companies and partnerships use phantom plans to reward key people while keeping the real cap table closed. Payout is typically cash tied to share value at a defined event or date.

## Taxation

For both instruments, the general rule is ordinary income at settlement, equal to the cash or share value received, reported as wages and subject to employment tax withholding.

## Professional detail

The cash-versus-stock settlement decision has a major accounting consequence under ASC 718. A cash-settled SAR or phantom unit is classified as a liability: it is measured at fair value at grant and then remeasured at fair value every reporting period through settlement, so expense swings with the stock price and the final expense equals the actual cash paid. A stock-settled SAR that settles in a fixed share equivalent is generally equity-classified, measured once at grant-date fair value and not remeasured. Because these awards commonly pay out well after the right to the payment vests, they sit squarely within IRC § 409A. To avoid its punitive tax  -  immediate income inclusion plus a 20% additional tax and premium interest  -  a SAR must be granted at or above fair market value on stock of the service recipient with no additional deferral feature to qualify for the § 409A exemption, while phantom plans must specify permissible payment events and fixed times at the outset. Valuation of the underlying stock in private companies should be supportable, often via a § 409A appraisal.
