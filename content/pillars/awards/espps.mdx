## The ESPP structure

An employee stock purchase plan (ESPP) is a company-sponsored program that allows eligible employees to purchase employer stock, typically at a discount, using accumulated payroll deductions. ESPPs operate on a cycle of offering periods - usually six or twelve months - during which employees contribute a percentage of their salary. At the end of each offering period, the accumulated funds are used to purchase shares at the designated purchase price.

Congress established a favorable tax regime for ESPPs in IRC Section 423, modeled loosely on the ISO framework. Plans that comply with Section 423 are called "qualified" or "statutory" ESPPs and offer employees capital gains treatment on a portion of their gains. Plans that do not meet Section 423 requirements are "non-qualified" ESPPs, which function more like NSO grants: the discount at purchase is ordinary income, and there is no lookback feature unless the plan provides one.

## Section 423 qualified plan requirements

To obtain the favorable tax treatment under IRC § 423, the plan must meet all of the following requirements:

**Stockholder approval.** The plan must be approved by shareholders within twelve months before or after the board adopts it.

**Equal rights and privileges.** All employees of the corporation (and participating subsidiaries) must be granted the same rights and privileges under the plan. However, the plan may exclude employees who have been employed less than two years, part-time or temporary employees, and employees who own 5% or more of the employer's stock.

**Non-transferability.** Purchase rights may not be transferred other than by will or intestacy laws.

**Maximum discount.** The purchase price may not be less than the lesser of 85% of FMV at the start of the offering period or 85% of FMV at the purchase date.

**Maximum offering period.** No offering period may exceed 27 months (or 5 years if the price is set at a fixed formula based solely on offering-date FMV).

**$25,000 accrual limit.** No employee may accrue the right to purchase more than $25,000 of stock annually.

## The lookback feature

The most powerful feature of a well-designed Section 423 ESPP is the lookback. Under the 85%/85% rule, the purchase price is the lower of 85% of the offering-date stock price or 85% of the purchase-date stock price. This means that if the stock rises over the offering period, employees purchase at 85% of the lower offering-date price - capturing not just the discount but also the appreciation.

Consider a 12-month offering period where stock starts at $20 and closes at $30. Employees purchase at $17 (85% of $20), representing an effective discount of $13 - or 43% - on the day-of-purchase price. If the stock instead falls to $15, employees purchase at $12.75 (85% of $15), capturing a 15% discount on current value.

This asymmetric benefit - participants always buy at the lower price - is what makes ESPPs with lookbacks particularly valuable to employees.

## Tax treatment: qualifying dispositions

For Section 423 plan shares, the tax treatment depends on whether the employee meets both statutory holding periods:
- More than **two years** from the first day of the offering period (offering date), and
- More than **one year** from the purchase date.

If both tests are met:
- The ordinary income component is the **lesser of** (a) the actual discount from offering-date FMV (i.e., FMV at offering date minus purchase price), or (b) the total gain (sale price minus purchase price).
- All remaining gain is long-term capital gain.
- The ordinary income is reported on Form W-2 in the year of sale, not the year of purchase.
- The company reports the share transfer on Form 3922 in the year of purchase.

## Tax treatment: disqualifying dispositions

A disqualifying disposition occurs when shares are sold before satisfying either holding period. In a disqualifying disposition:
- The spread at purchase (FMV on purchase date minus purchase price) is ordinary income in the year of sale.
- The employer must report this on Form W-2 and may be required to withhold, though some administrators handle ESPP disqualifying dispositions through supplemental payroll runs.
- Any gain above the ordinary income component is capital gain (short-term or long-term depending on the holding period from purchase to sale).

Disqualifying dispositions are common because employees often sell ESPP shares immediately to capture the guaranteed discount rather than holding for the qualifying period.

## Non-qualified ESPPs

Non-qualified ESPPs are not subject to the Section 423 requirements and therefore have more design flexibility. They can provide different benefits to different employee groups, can be offered to contractors or non-employees, and need not receive stockholder approval. However, they offer no preferential tax treatment: the discount at purchase is ordinary income subject to payroll withholding, similar to an NSO exercise.

## Enrollment and contribution mechanics

During open enrollment windows, eligible employees elect a contribution percentage (typically 1 - 15% of eligible pay, subject to plan limits). Contributions are deducted from each paycheck and accumulated in a notional account. At the end of the offering period (the purchase date), accumulated contributions are applied to purchase shares.

Employees may typically withdraw contributions before the purchase date - common when stock prices fall significantly during the offering period. Most plans allow a single withdrawal per offering period; some allow unlimited changes.

## Form 3922 reporting

For Section 423 ESPPs, the company must file Form 3922 with the IRS for each employee who first acquires stock under the plan during the calendar year. The form captures the offering date, purchase date, offering-date FMV, purchase-date FMV, purchase price paid, and number of shares transferred. This data is essential for employees to correctly calculate their gain and character in the year of sale.
