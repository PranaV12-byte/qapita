## What exercise means

Exercising a stock option is the act of electing to purchase the underlying shares at the exercise price specified in the option agreement. Until exercise, the option holder has only a right—not an obligation—to buy shares. Exercise converts that right into actual share ownership. Once shares are purchased, the option is extinguished and the holder becomes a stockholder (subject to whatever restrictions apply to the shares).

Exercise is only available for vested options. Unvested options cannot be exercised unless the plan provides for early exercise, which is a separate feature and subject to its own restrictions.

## Exercise methods

### Cash exercise
The option holder sends a check or wire for the full exercise price to the company or its transfer agent, and the company issues shares in return. This method requires the participant to have sufficient liquid funds and results in the participant owning shares with no immediate proceeds.

**When it makes sense:** When the participant wants to hold shares for long-term gain, has liquidity available, and the exercise price is low relative to the amount they can afford.

### Cashless exercise (same-day sale)
The participant authorizes a broker to advance the exercise price, the company issues shares, the broker immediately sells all shares in the open market, and the proceeds are used to repay the broker and cover withholding taxes. The participant receives net cash proceeds.

**When it makes sense:** When the participant needs liquidity, does not want to hold shares, or does not have funds for a cash exercise.

**ISO caution:** A same-day sale of ISO shares in the year of exercise constitutes a disqualifying disposition, eliminating ISO tax benefits. Participants exercising ISOs who want qualifying-disposition treatment must hold shares past year-end.

### Sell-to-cover
The participant exercises and simultaneously sells only enough shares to cover the exercise price and withholding taxes. The remaining shares are held in the participant's brokerage account. This is a hybrid approach.

### Net exercise (net settlement)
Instead of paying the exercise price in cash, the company delivers a net number of shares equal to the option's intrinsic value. The formula is: **Net shares = (Market Price − Exercise Price) × Shares Exercised / Market Price**. No cash changes hands; the participant simply receives fewer shares. Net exercise is often used for private company exercises or for convenience in public company plans.

### Stock swap
The participant delivers already-owned shares of company stock as payment for the exercise price. The value of the surrendered shares must equal the exercise price. The surrendered shares must typically have been held long enough to avoid a disqualifying disposition if they are ISO shares.

## Timing considerations for ISOs

The ISO exercise timing decision is more complex than for NSOs because:

1. **AMT exposure:** Exercising ISOs and holding shares creates an AMT preference item. If the spread is large, the resulting AMT can be significant.

2. **Qualifying disposition clock:** The two-year grant/one-year exercise holding periods begin running upon exercise. Exercising early—when the spread is small—means the employee reaches qualifying-disposition status sooner and with lower ordinary income exposure.

3. **Same-year sale rule:** Exercising and selling ISO shares in the same calendar year eliminates the AMT preference but is a disqualifying disposition. This may actually produce a better after-tax result than holding if the employee's regular tax rate is lower than their AMT rate.

4. **Section 422(d) limit:** No more than $100,000 of options (by grant-date FMV) can first become exercisable in any year under ISO treatment. This doesn't limit when options can be exercised, but it affects which tranches qualify as ISOs versus NSOs.

## Post-termination exercise windows

After a participant terminates employment, vested options can generally be exercised during a specified post-termination exercise period (PTEP). Key rules:

- **ISOs:** Must be exercised within 3 months of termination to retain ISO status (12 months for disability; no statutory window for death, though the plan may specify one). Options not exercised within 3 months automatically convert to NSOs for tax purposes.
- **NSOs:** No statutory time limit, but the plan document specifies the window—commonly 90 days for voluntary or involuntary termination (other than cause) and immediate cancellation for termination for cause.

Administrators must notify departing employees of their PTEP and the date by which options must be exercised. The termination date used to start the PTEP should be the last day of employment, not the date any notice is given.

## Blackout periods and trading restrictions

Public company employees may be restricted from exercising options during blackout periods—typically the period around quarterly earnings announcements. Additionally, Section 16 officers cannot sell shares acquired upon exercise within six months of a prior offsetting transaction under Section 16(b). These restrictions can prevent exercise even when options are about to expire.

Administrators should maintain a blackout calendar and communicate it clearly to option holders. Participants with options nearing expiration during a blackout may need alternative arrangements (such as a pre-established 10b5-1 plan) to ensure they can exercise before expiration.

## Processing an exercise: administrator checklist

1. Verify the option is vested and within its exercise window.
2. Confirm the participant is not subject to a blackout or trading restriction.
3. Collect the exercise price using the elected payment method.
4. Calculate withholding for NSOs: spread × applicable withholding rates.
5. Issue shares to the participant's designated brokerage account or record in the cap table.
6. For ISOs: no withholding at exercise, but document the FMV and spread for Form 3921.
7. Update the plan administration system and option ledger.
8. File Form 3921 (ISOs) or process Form W-2 supplemental payroll (NSOs) on the appropriate schedule.
