## What participants need to receive

Participant communications are the bridge between a technical equity plan and the employees who hold awards. The foundational documents are the plan document itself, which sets the rules the board adopted, and the individual grant notice and award agreement, which state the specific terms of each award: type, share count, price, grant date, and vesting schedule. Employees should be able to find these easily, typically through an equity portal that also shows current holdings, vested and unvested totals, and transaction history.

Beyond the legal documents, good communication includes plain-language education. Materials might explain the difference between options and restricted stock units, what vesting means, how an ESPP purchase works, and what generally happens at exercise, release, or sale. For employee stock purchase plans, communication is time-sensitive: enrollment windows open and close on set dates, so administrators must announce them early and confirm elections before each offering period begins.

## Disclosures and the advice boundary

A recurring theme across all participant messaging is the boundary on advice. The company can and should explain how the plan operates, but it must not tell an individual employee what to do about their taxes or investments. Materials should say plainly that the company does not provide personal tax, legal, or financial advice and that participants should consult their own advisors.

## Professional detail

Securities law drives much of the required disclosure. When a public company registers shares issuable under a plan on a Form S-8 registration statement, it must deliver a plan prospectus  -  the Section 10(a) prospectus  -  to participants describing the plan and the securities offered, and keep it current as the plan is amended. Private companies commonly rely on Securities Act Rule 701 for compensatory grants; once aggregate sales during a rolling 12-month period exceed the specified threshold, Rule 701 requires enhanced disclosure to recipients, including a summary of material plan terms, risk factors, and financial statements provided a reasonable time before the awards.

Statutory plans carry their own communication needs. ESPPs intended to qualify under IRC Section 423 and incentive stock options under Sections 421 through 424 have specific rules on eligibility, holding periods, and disqualifying dispositions that participants should understand, even though the company still avoids individualized advice. Clear, accurate, and timely education reduces confusion and support burden while helping employees appreciate the value of their equity.
