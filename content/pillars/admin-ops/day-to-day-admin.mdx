## Keeping the plan running accurately

Day-to-day plan administration is the operational work that keeps an equity program accurate and defensible. It centers on clean data: every grant must be recorded with the correct grant date, share count, exercise or purchase price, vesting schedule, and award type. Small data errors compound quickly, because equity records feed compensation expense, cap table ownership, and tax reporting.

The core recurring tasks are processing new grants after board or committee approval, applying scheduled vesting events, executing exercises and RSU releases, and recording cancellations or forfeitures when employees leave. Terminations deserve particular care: administrators must apply the correct post-termination exercise window, forfeit unvested awards, and update outstanding balances promptly. Adjustments for stock splits, dividends, or corporate transactions must be applied consistently across all affected grants.

## Reconciliation and the system of record

A single system of record prevents conflicting numbers. Most companies designate their cap table or equity management platform as authoritative for outstanding awards, then reconcile it against the transfer agent, which maintains the registered share ledger for issued shares. Regular reconciliation, commonly monthly and always at quarter-end, confirms that granted, vested, exercised, forfeited, and outstanding totals agree across systems and tie to payroll and accounting.

## Professional detail

For public companies, plan administration operates inside internal control over financial reporting under Sarbanes-Oxley Sections 302 and 404. Controls typically include segregation of duties so that the person entering a grant is not the person approving it, documented review of vesting and exercise batches, and reconciliation sign-offs retained as audit evidence. Equity compensation expense recognized under ASC 718 depends directly on accurate grant-date fair values, vesting assumptions, and forfeiture data, so a data error is also a potential financial reporting error.

Information reporting obligations also flow from clean records. IRC Section 6039 requires furnishing information statements to employees and filing with the IRS for incentive stock option exercises and certain ESPP share transfers, so administrators must capture exercise and transfer details as they occur rather than reconstructing them at year-end. Maintaining an audit trail  -  who changed what, when, and with what approval  -  is essential both for the annual financial statement audit and for responding to any later inquiry about a specific award.
