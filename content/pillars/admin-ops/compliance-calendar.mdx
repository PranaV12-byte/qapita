## Why a calendar matters

Equity compliance is built on fixed dates, and many of them cluster at year-end and in the spring. A compliance calendar collects these recurring obligations in one place so nothing slips. Because several deadlines carry penalties or affect the validity of grants, administrators typically build the calendar early each year and assign an owner to every item.

The first wave hits in winter. Under IRC Section 6039, companies must furnish information statements to employees by January 31 for the prior year: Form 3921 for incentive stock option exercises and Form 3922 for the first transfer of legal title to ESPP shares acquired under a qualified Section 423 plan. The corresponding filings to the IRS are due by February 28 on paper or March 31 if filed electronically. Form W-2, which reports equity compensation income and withholding, is also due to employees by January 31.

## The rest of the year

Section 16 filings are event-driven rather than calendar-driven but must be tracked continuously: Form 3 within ten days of becoming an insider, Form 4 generally by the second business day after a reportable transaction, and Form 5 after year-end for certain items. Trading windows open and close around earnings, and any 10b5-1 plans should be logged with their cooling-off periods. Proxy season in the spring drives compensation disclosure, and Form S-8 registrations may need topping up as evergreen share reserves grow.

## Professional detail

The annual 409A valuation refresh anchors the private-company calendar. To set incentive stock option and nonqualified option exercise prices at fair market value, companies obtain an independent valuation and refresh it at least every 12 months, and sooner after a material event such as a new financing round or a significant change in the business. A valuation that meets the Section 409A safe-harbor conditions shifts the burden to the IRS to show the value was grossly unreasonable, which protects both the company and optionholders from the substantial taxes and penalties that a below-market exercise price on deferred compensation can trigger.

For public companies, the calendar also tracks Form S-8 registration of new plan shares, which must be effective before shares are issued, and the interplay of blackout periods with the equity-transaction schedule. Building buffer time before each statutory deadline, rather than working to the deadline itself, is the practical safeguard that keeps the program compliant year after year.
