## Basic versus diluted EPS

ASC 260 requires companies with public stock to present two earnings-per-share figures. Basic EPS divides income available to common shareholders by the weighted-average number of common shares actually outstanding during the period. Diluted EPS expands the denominator to include the additional shares that would exist if all dilutive potential common shares, such as outstanding options, stock appreciation rights, restricted stock units, and convertible instruments, were converted into common stock. The purpose is to show shareholders the maximum potential dilution of their ownership.

Equity compensation is a principal source of potential common shares. Because these instruments can increase the share count without a proportional increase in income, they generally reduce diluted EPS relative to basic EPS.

## The treasury stock method

For options and SARs, ASC 260 prescribes the treasury stock method. The method assumes that the instruments are exercised at the start of the period (or issuance date, if later) and that the company uses the assumed proceeds to repurchase its own shares at the average market price for the period. Only the net increase, shares deemed issued on exercise minus shares deemed repurchased, is added to the diluted denominator.

Assumed proceeds comprise the exercise price the holder pays plus the average amount of unrecognized compensation cost that has not yet been expensed. Including unrecognized cost reflects that the company still receives value in the form of future services. When the exercise price plus unrecognized cost exceeds the average market price, the calculation would add shares that increase EPS, an antidilutive result, so those awards are simply excluded.

## Antidilution and contingent shares

An instrument is included in diluted EPS only if it is dilutive, meaning it reduces EPS or increases loss per share. In a net-loss period, all potential common shares are antidilutive and excluded, so basic and diluted EPS are equal. Awards are tested individually, and management may not include antidilutive instruments to smooth results.

Performance-based awards raise a timing question. Contingently issuable shares, such as PSUs, enter the diluted calculation only when the contingency would be met if the measurement date were the end of the reporting period. Participating securities that carry nonforfeitable dividend rights require the two-class method, which allocates earnings between common stock and those securities before computing EPS.

## Professional detail

The two-class method treats participating securities, for example nonvested restricted stock with nonforfeitable dividends, as a separate class of equity. Undistributed earnings are allocated to common and participating shares according to their respective participation rights, reducing income attributable to common shareholders for both basic and diluted EPS.

Diluted EPS uses a year-to-date weighting that considers dilution for each interim period; the annual figure is not simply the sum or average of quarters, and antidilutive quarters cannot offset dilutive ones.

Because unrecognized compensation cost declines as awards vest, the assumed proceeds for a given option shrink over time, so the same award typically becomes more dilutive in later periods.
