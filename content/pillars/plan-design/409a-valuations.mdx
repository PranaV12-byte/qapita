## Why a 409A valuation exists

Private companies have no public market price, yet they must still set a defensible fair market value (FMV) for their common stock before granting stock options. Section 409A of the Internal Revenue Code treats an option granted with an exercise price below FMV on the grant date as a form of deferred compensation, exposing the recipient to harsh tax consequences. A 409A valuation is an independent appraisal that establishes common-stock FMV so the company can price options at or above that figure and keep them outside the deferred-compensation rules.

## The safe harbors

The regulations provide safe-harbor methods that, if followed, create a presumption the FMV determination was reasonable, shifting the burden to the IRS to prove otherwise. The most common is the independent-appraisal presumption: a valuation performed by a qualified independent appraiser is presumed reasonable for stock valued as of a date within the prior twelve months, provided no material change has occurred. Additional safe harbors cover valuations set by a binding formula and a specific method for illiquid stock of a start-up corporation valued by someone with adequate knowledge and experience.

## Consequences of underpricing

If an option is under-priced and no exception applies, the holder faces income recognition as the award vests, a 20 percent additional federal tax under Section 409A, and possible premium interest, often with state-level penalties on top. Because the burden and cost fall largely on employees, maintaining current valuations is a core compliance obligation for the issuer.

## Professional detail

The twelve-month presumption is not a fixed calendar entitlement. A material event, a priced equity financing, a significant acquisition or divestiture, a major shift in projections, or preparation for a liquidity event, can render an existing valuation stale and require a refresh before the anniversary. Companies granting options during a period of rapid change should re-examine FMV more frequently.

The 409A appraisal also intersects with financial reporting. The same common-stock value that anchors the exercise price feeds into the grant-date fair value measured under ASC 718 for stock-based compensation expense, though the accounting fair value of an option is derived separately using an option-pricing model. Divergence between a low 409A common-stock value and a higher value implied by a recent preferred financing can attract auditor scrutiny under the "cheap stock" analysis, particularly in the runway before an initial public offering.

Incentive stock options carry a parallel requirement: Section 422 conditions favorable ISO treatment on the exercise price being set at no less than FMV on the grant date, determined in good faith, which the 409A appraisal supports.
