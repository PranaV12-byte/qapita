## From options to full-value awards

The center of gravity in equity design has moved away from stock options toward restricted stock units and performance-based units. Options only reward participants when the share price climbs above the exercise price, so a flat or declining market can leave them worthless. Full-value awards deliver shares outright on vesting, so they retain value across market cycles, convey a given dollar amount using fewer shares, and are easier for employees to value. Public companies increasingly split executive equity between time-vesting RSUs and performance stock units (PSUs).

## Performance metrics

PSUs vest based on the achievement of defined goals over a multi-year period, commonly three years. Relative total shareholder return (TSR) measured against a peer group or index is among the most widely used metrics because it directly links payout to market performance versus competitors. Companies also anchor awards to financial measures such as revenue growth, earnings per share, operating margin, return on invested capital, or cash flow. Some issuers weight several metrics together and set threshold, target, and maximum payout levels.

## Governance features

Design now routinely incorporates governance provisions. Double-trigger vesting has become the norm for change-in-control protection, replacing automatic single-trigger acceleration that investors view unfavorably. Stock ownership guidelines require executives to hold shares worth a multiple of salary, and post-vest or post-exercise holding requirements keep them invested for a period. A growing number of programs also embed ESG or human-capital metrics, such as safety, emissions, or workforce goals, typically as a modifier or a small weighted component rather than the primary driver.

## Professional detail

Clawback policies moved from voluntary to mandatory. Listing standards adopted under the Dodd-Frank Act require issuers to maintain a written policy recovering incentive-based compensation received by current and former executive officers during the three fiscal years preceding a required accounting restatement, to the extent that pay exceeded what the corrected figures would have supported. Recovery is generally no-fault and applies to compensation tied to financial reporting measures, including performance equity.

Award-design choices carry accounting consequences under ASC 718. Awards with market conditions such as relative TSR are valued using a Monte Carlo simulation at grant, and that grant-date fair value is expensed over the service period regardless of whether the market condition is ultimately met. Awards with performance conditions tied to internal metrics are expensed based on the probable outcome, with expense trued up as the estimate of achievement changes. This asymmetry influences which metrics companies select.
