## The authorized share pool

Every equity incentive plan is anchored to a finite pool of shares that shareholders have approved for issuance. Awards may only be granted while shares remain in that reserve, so tracking the pool is one of the central administrative duties under any plan. The reserve is set in the plan document as an absolute number, and increasing it generally requires a new shareholder vote.

Some plans include an evergreen provision that automatically adds shares each year, typically a fixed count or a percentage of the outstanding share count. Evergreen features are more common in newly public companies but tend to draw criticism from institutional investors because they permit ongoing dilution without a fresh approval.

## Counting and recycling rules

Not every granted share reduces the pool one-for-one for the plan's life. Share-counting rules define what happens when awards are forfeited, cancelled, or settled net of taxes. Under a liberal ("full") recycling regime, shares from awards that never deliver value return to the pool. Stricter plans prohibit adding back shares withheld to cover the exercise price or tax withholding.

Many plans apply a fungible share ratio, charging full-value awards such as restricted stock units against the pool at a higher rate than options or stock appreciation rights (for example, counting each RSU as more than one share). This reflects the greater economic value delivered by a full-value award.

## Per-award and per-person limits

To preserve incentive stock option treatment, the plan must cap the total shares issuable as ISOs. Separately, many plans historically imposed per-person annual limits, a practice driven largely by the former performance-based exception under Section 162(m); although that exception was repealed for most public companies, sub-limits still appear for governance reasons.

## Professional detail

Listing standards under the NYSE and Nasdaq require shareholder approval before a company adopts an equity compensation plan or materially increases the shares reserved under one, subject to narrow exceptions (such as certain inducement grants and tax-qualified plans). Material revisions include expanding the share pool, adding an evergreen, extending the term, or repricing where the plan did not previously permit it.

Proxy advisory firms evaluate a proposed share request against a model that weighs cost (often a shareholder-value-transfer calculation) alongside three-year average burn rate relative to industry peers, plan features (repricing without approval, liberal recycling, single-trigger acceleration, evergreen terms), and grant practices. A request scoring poorly on these factors can trigger an against recommendation.

Administrators should reconcile the reserve continuously: subtract gross awards granted, apply the fungible ratio for full-value awards, and add back only those shares the counting rules permit. Misapplying add-backs is a common source of over-issuance risk.
