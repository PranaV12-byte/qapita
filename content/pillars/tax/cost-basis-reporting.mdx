## The reporting chain

Selling shares acquired through equity compensation generates two documents. Compensation income  -  the spread taxed at vest, exercise, or a disqualifying disposition  -  appears in your wages on Form W-2. The later sale of those shares appears on Form 1099-B, which your broker files and sends to you.

## The double-taxation trap

The core problem is that these two forms are not reconciled by the broker. Under the cost-basis reporting rules, brokers generally report to the IRS only the amount you actually paid for equity-comp shares  -  often just the option exercise price or ESPP purchase price. They are not required to include the compensation income that was already added to your W-2. As a result, the basis on the 1099-B is understated.

If you simply copy that low basis onto your return, your reported capital gain is inflated by the exact amount already taxed as wages  -  so the same dollars are taxed twice. This is one of the most common and costly equity-comp filing errors.

## Correcting it on Form 8949

The fix is a basis adjustment on Form 8949. You start with the broker's reported proceeds and reported basis, then adjust the basis upward to your true, full basis: the amount paid plus the compensation income recognized on your W-2. The corrected gain or loss then flows to Schedule D. Brokerage supplemental statements often show the adjusted basis to help, even when the 1099-B itself does not.

## W-2 versus 1099-B

Keeping the roles straight matters: the W-2 captures the ordinary compensation element, while the 1099-B captures the capital gain or loss on disposition measured from full basis. Confusing the two is what produces the double count.

## Professional detail

Two information returns support basis and holding-period tracking. Form 3921 is issued for each ISO exercise, capturing the exercise date, exercise price, and fair market value at exercise  -  data needed for both regular tax basis and alternative minimum tax calculations. Form 3922 is issued on the first transfer of stock acquired under a qualified ESPP, capturing purchase-date and grant-date values used to determine the compensation element on a later sale.

Neither form reports income by itself. Instead they arm the taxpayer to compute basis correctly, distinguish qualifying from disqualifying dispositions, and support the Form 8949 adjustment that avoids double taxation.
