## What 409A covers

Section 409A regulates nonqualified deferred compensation  -  broadly, a legally binding right to compensation in one year that is or may be paid in a later year. Its aim is to limit discretion over the timing of income. Although often associated with executive deferral plans, 409A also reaches certain equity arrangements, which is why it matters throughout equity-comp design.

## The stock-option exemption

Most stock options are structured to fall outside 409A. An option on the employer's common stock is exempt when its exercise price is at least the fair market value of the underlying stock on the grant date, the number of shares is fixed, and it carries no additional deferral feature. This exemption is why setting an accurate grant-date fair market value is so important  -  particularly for private companies, which obtain independent 409A valuations to support the strike price.

## When equity falls inside 409A

Trouble arises when an option is granted at a discount to fair market value: the built-in spread is treated as deferred compensation and the option becomes subject to 409A. Certain RSU structures can also be caught  -  for example, when settlement can be deferred beyond vesting rather than paid promptly. In those cases the award must satisfy 409A's rules on election timing and payment events.

## Permissible timing

For amounts inside 409A, payment may generally be made only on specified permissible events: a fixed date or schedule, separation from service, death, disability, a change in control, or an unforeseeable emergency. Deferral elections must be made before the compensation is earned, and accelerating payment is broadly prohibited.

## Professional detail

The penalties for a 409A failure fall on the service provider, not the employer, and are severe. On a violation, all deferred compensation under the arrangement that is vested becomes includible in income immediately, regardless of whether it has actually been paid.

On top of ordinary income tax, the individual owes a 20% additional federal tax on the includible amount, plus a premium interest charge computed as if the income had been recognized when it first vested. Several states layer their own additional tax on 409A violations. Because the consequences are so punitive and land on employees, companies invest heavily in compliant plan documents, defensible grant-date valuations, and disciplined administration of deferral and payment elections.
