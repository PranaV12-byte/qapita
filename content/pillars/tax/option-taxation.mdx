## The two option types and their tax frameworks

Stock options granted in employment contexts are either incentive stock options (ISOs), governed by IRC § 422, or non-qualified stock options (NSOs), governed by the general principles of IRC § 83. The fundamental distinction is when and how the option spread is taxed:

- **NSOs:** Spread is ordinary income at exercise. Subject to income tax, Social Security, and Medicare. Company gets a deduction. Straightforward.
- **ISOs:** No ordinary income at exercise (for regular tax purposes). Instead, the spread is an AMT preference item. If you hold qualifying-disposition requirements, the entire gain from exercise price to sale price is long-term capital gain.

## NSO taxation in detail

### At grant
No taxable event. NSOs without a readily ascertainable FMV (the vast majority) are not taxable when granted.

### At exercise
The spread—FMV on the exercise date minus the exercise price—is ordinary compensation income. If the spread is $50 per share and you exercise 1,000 shares, you have $50,000 of ordinary income. This appears on your W-2 and is subject to:
- Federal income tax at your marginal rate (up to 37% in 2024–2025)
- Social Security tax at 6.2% on wages up to the Social Security wage base ($168,600 in 2024)
- Medicare at 1.45% (plus an additional 0.9% if total wages exceed $200,000 for single filers, or $250,000 for married filing jointly)

Your employer must withhold supplemental income tax (22% flat for most employees; 37% for wages above $1 million in the year).

### After exercise
Your cost basis in the shares is the FMV on the exercise date. Gain or loss from that basis to the eventual sale price is capital gain or loss. Holding the shares for more than 12 months from exercise generates long-term capital gain; selling within 12 months is short-term gain at ordinary rates.

## ISO taxation in detail

### At grant
No taxable event. Same as NSOs.

### At exercise—regular tax
Under IRC § 422, no ordinary income is recognized when an ISO is exercised. The option holder receives shares without any W-2 income. The employer does not withhold FICA or income tax.

### At exercise—AMT
Under IRC § 56(b)(3), the ISO spread is an adjustment to alternative minimum taxable income. The spread increases your AMTI, and the AMT is calculated on AMTI after the AMT exemption. For 2024, the AMT exemption is $85,700 for single filers and $133,300 for married filing jointly, phasing out at higher AMTI levels.

If your tentative minimum tax (AMTI × 26% up to $220,700; 28% above) exceeds your regular tax, you owe the difference as AMT. Large ISO spreads in high-valuation years can generate substantial AMT bills even before the employee has sold any shares—creating a situation where taxes are owed on paper gains.

### Qualifying disposition
If you hold ISO shares for more than two years from the grant date AND more than one year from the exercise date, any sale is a qualifying disposition. The entire gain from exercise price to sale price is long-term capital gain. There is no ordinary income. The employer does not report a W-2 inclusion (although Form 3921 must still be filed).

### Disqualifying disposition
If you sell ISO shares before meeting both holding periods, the ordinary income component equals the lesser of:
- The spread at exercise (FMV at exercise minus exercise price), or
- The actual gain realized (sale price minus exercise price)

This ordinary income is reported on your W-2 in the year of sale (not the year of exercise), and all remaining gain is capital gain.

## The AMT credit

AMT paid because of ISO adjustments—not because of other AMT preference items—generates a minimum tax credit (MTC) under IRC § 53. The credit carryforward reduces regular tax in future years when regular tax exceeds the tentative minimum tax.

**Example:** An employee pays $20,000 of AMT in Year 1 due to an ISO exercise. In Year 2, her regular tax liability is $35,000 and her tentative minimum tax is $10,000. She can apply $25,000 of MTC (the excess of regular over tentative minimum tax) to reduce her Year 2 regular tax from $35,000 to $10,000. The remaining MTC carries to Year 3 and beyond.

## Planning strategies for ISO exercises

**Exercise early in the year.** If you plan to hold for a qualifying disposition, exercising in January gives you the full year to decide whether to sell before December 31 without creating a disqualifying disposition in the exercise year.

**Model AMT before exercising.** Use the current year's AMT exemption and phaseout thresholds to estimate whether an exercise will trigger AMT. Factors include your W-2 income, deductions, and the size of the ISO spread.

**Consider a partial same-year sale.** If the AMT bill would be unmanageable, selling some ISO shares before December 31 of the exercise year creates a disqualifying disposition on those shares (eliminating their AMT preference) while preserving the qualifying-disposition path for the remaining shares.

**Track cost basis for both regular tax and AMT.** For AMT purposes, the cost basis of ISO shares is the FMV at exercise (not the exercise price), because the spread was already included in AMTI. Keeping two sets of records avoids double-taxation when the shares are eventually sold.
