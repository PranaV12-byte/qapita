## RSU taxation: timing and mechanics

Restricted stock units generate ordinary income when shares are delivered to the participant - in most cases, the vesting date. At the moment of delivery, the full fair market value of shares delivered is compensation income under IRC § 83(a), included in gross income in the year of delivery.

### The vesting taxable event

When RSU shares vest, the administrator must:
1. Determine the FMV of the shares on the vesting date (closing market price for public company shares).
2. Calculate the withholding obligation: FMV × number of shares vesting × applicable withholding rates.
3. Execute the chosen withholding method (typically net share withholding).
4. Report the income through payroll.

The income recognized equals the number of shares vesting multiplied by the FMV on the vesting date. This amount is subject to:
- **Federal income tax withholding:** 22% supplemental rate on amounts up to $1 million in the year; 37% above.
- **Social Security tax:** 6.2% on wages up to the annual wage base.
- **Medicare tax:** 1.45% on all wages, plus 0.9% additional Medicare tax on wages exceeding $200,000 for single filers.
- **State income tax:** Varies by state. Multi-state employees may owe tax in multiple states based on where they worked during the vesting period.

### W-2 reporting

RSU income is included in Box 1 (Total Wages) and Box 3 and Box 5 (Social Security and Medicare wages) of Form W-2. The value of RSUs (gross income before tax withholding) must also be reported in Box 12 with Code V (for restricted stock, the gross income from RSU delivery).

## Net share withholding in practice

Net share withholding is the dominant RSU withholding method for public companies. Here is how the transaction flows:

1. On the vesting date, the plan administration system identifies the vesting grant and calculates the tax withholding amount.
2. The administrator instructs the custodian broker to withhold X shares (where X = withholding amount / FMV per share, rounded up).
3. The broker reacquires those shares at the current market price.
4. The broker remits the proceeds to the employer (or to the IRS via the employer's payroll tax account).
5. The remaining shares are delivered to the participant's brokerage account.

The participant receives a net share quantity. From the participant's perspective, they see shares appear in their account on vest date, with a cost basis equal to the FMV on that date (because the ordinary income has already been recognized on that day).

## Post-vesting capital gain

After shares are delivered at vesting, the participant's basis is the FMV on the delivery date. Any appreciation or depreciation from that point is capital gain or loss:
- If held more than 12 months from delivery, long-term capital gain rates apply (0%, 15%, or 20% depending on taxable income).
- If sold within 12 months, short-term gain at ordinary income rates.
- If shares decline in value after vesting, the participant has a capital loss - but cannot recover the income taxes already paid on the vesting-date FMV.

## ESPP taxation: qualifying versus disqualifying dispositions

ESPP tax treatment splits into two regimes depending on whether the participant meets both statutory holding periods:
- More than **2 years** from the first day of the offering period (the "offering date"), AND
- More than **1 year** from the purchase date.

### Qualifying disposition

When both holding periods are satisfied, the tax treatment is favorable:

**Ordinary income:** The lesser of:
(a) The actual discount from the offering-date price: (Offering-date FMV − Purchase price) × shares sold, or
(b) Total gain realized: (Sale price − Purchase price) × shares sold.

**Long-term capital gain:** Any remaining gain above the ordinary income component.

**Example:** Offering date FMV $20, purchase price $17 (85% × $20), sale price $35 two years later.
- Ordinary income = lesser of ($20 − $17) × shares or ($35 − $17) × shares = $3/share ordinary income.
- Long-term capital gain = ($35 − $20) × shares = $15/share.
- W-2 reporting in the year of sale.

### Disqualifying disposition

When shares are sold before satisfying either holding period (typically an immediate-sale after purchase):

**Ordinary income:** The spread at purchase = (FMV on purchase date − Purchase price) × shares sold. This is regardless of whether the shares are sold at a loss afterward.

**Capital gain or loss:** Sale price minus FMV on purchase date. Short-term if held less than 12 months from purchase; long-term if held more than 12 months.

**Example:** Offering date FMV $20, purchase date FMV $28, purchase price $17 (85% × $20), same-day sale at $28.
- Ordinary income = ($28 − $17) × shares = $11/share.
- Capital gain = $0 (sold at purchase-date FMV).

### W-2 and withholding for ESPP

Unlike RSU vesting, ESPP purchases themselves do not trigger withholding - participants purchase shares using after-tax payroll deductions. The withholding obligation arises only when a disqualifying disposition is reported:
- If a disqualifying disposition occurs, the ordinary income element must be reported on the participant's W-2. Some administrators handle this through a supplemental payroll run in the year the disqualifying disposition is reported.
- Qualifying disposition ordinary income is also reported on W-2 in the year of sale but typically without withholding.

The Form 3922 (filed by the company in the year of purchase) provides participants with the data needed to correctly compute their tax at the time of sale: offering date, purchase date, offering-date FMV, purchase-date FMV, and purchase price.
